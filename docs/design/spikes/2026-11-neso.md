## 1. Answers in brief

The backlog item asked for the regional endpoint path, the region ids, and whether actual regional values exist.

1. **Regional path.** `GET /regional/intensity/{from}/{to}/regionid/{regionid}` for one region, `.../postcode/{postcode}` for an outward postcode, and `GET /regional/intensity/{from}/{to}` for every region. **[API]**
2. **Region ids.** 1 to 14 are the DNO regions, 15 is England, 16 Scotland, 17 Wales and 18 GB. The full list is in section 5.1. **[API]** The API definition lists 1 to 17 only. **[DOC]**
3. **Actual regional values do not exist.** Regional values are forecasts only, for past periods as well as future ones. The regional `intensity` object has `forecast` and `index` and never `actual` **[API]**, the regional schemas in the API definition define no actual **[DOC]**, and the regional methodology paper describes forecasts only **[DOC]**. Actual values exist only in the national series. The web search of 2026-11-10 is confirmed.
4. **What that means in practice.** For past periods the regional numbers behave like a near-real-time estimate, not a day-ahead forecast: the regional GB aggregate sits within about 4 gCO2/kWh of the national actual. The larger effect is the location: London's regional series averaged 25 % above the national actual over the first half of October 2026. Section 5.4 has the numbers. **[INFERRED]** from recorded data.
5. **National series.** `GET /intensity/{from}/{to}`, half hours in UTC, under 31 days per request, first period 2017-09-11T23:00Z, a missing actual is `null`. Section 4. **[API]**
6. **How soon values settle.** A national actual first appears 12 to 14 minutes after its half hour ends and can change by a gram when the next half hour is published, about 43 minutes after the end. Nothing older changed in 70 minutes of watching. The response has no marker for a preliminary value, so a source has to derive one from the property it read and the age of the period. Sections 4.5 and 4.6. **[API]**; that a value is settled after its second publication is **[INFERRED]**.
7. **Licence.** CC BY 4.0. NESO asks for no particular attribution wording. Section 7. **[DOC]**
8. **Recommendation.** Map the four GB cloud regions to the national actual series, not to a regional series. Section 8.

Two findings contradict the design document: the national series starts on 2017-09-11, not 2017-09-26, and NESO's figures are consumption-based with direct CO2 factors, not production-based.

## 4. National intensity over a date range

### 4.1 Request and fields

`GET /intensity/{from}/{to}` returns:

```json
{
  "data": [
    {
      "from": "2026-11-03T00:00Z",
      "to": "2026-11-03T00:30Z",
      "intensity": { "forecast": 133, "actual": 128, "index": "moderate" }
    }
  ]
}
```

- `from`, `to`: the half hour, UTC, minute precision, literal `Z`.
- `intensity.forecast`: integer, gCO2/kWh. Present in every period seen, past and future.
- `intensity.actual`: integer gCO2/kWh, or `null`. The API definition calls it the estimated actual: it is computed from metered generation, with estimates for embedded wind and solar. **[DOC]**
- `intensity.index`: `very low`, `low`, `moderate`, `high` or `very high`. The bands tighten each year (methodology table 2), so the index is not comparable across years. Not needed by the source.

All 3,960 past half hours sampled had integer values. **[API]** The unit is CO2, not CO2e (section 7.4).

### 4.2 Period convention

- Every period is 30 minutes, starts at minute 0 or 30 UTC, and ends where the next begins, so a period is `[from, to)`. Checked on 4,061 periods in four ranges with no gap or overlap, including 2026-10-25, the day the UK clocks went back, which has 48 periods in UTC. **[API]**
- All times are UTC. **[DOC]** Confirmed with a summer date. **[API]**
- A range request returns every half hour whose **end** lies between `from` and `to`, both inclusive. An aligned request therefore returns one more period than the range holds: the half hour that ends at `from`. `/intensity/2026-11-03T00:00Z/2026-11-03T02:00Z` returns five periods, the first being 23:30Z to 00:00Z. **[API]** The rule held in every national range response recorded.
- Moving `from` one minute later removes the extra period: `/intensity/2026-11-03T00:01Z/2026-11-03T01:15Z` returns exactly 00:00Z to 00:30Z and 00:30Z to 01:00Z. The half hour containing 01:15Z is left out because it ends after `to`. **[API]**
- `fw24h`, `fw48h` and `pt24h` follow the same rule (49, 97 and 49 periods). `/intensity/{from}` with an aligned `from` returns the half hour that ends there. **[API]**
- The date endpoints select by UK local day, although the timestamps they return are UTC. `/intensity/date/2026-07-01` returns 2026-06-30T23:00Z to 2026-07-01T23:00Z, and settlement period 1 of that date starts at 23:00Z the day before. **[API]** The source should not use them: the resolver works in UTC days, and a local day has 46 or 50 half hours when the clocks change **[INFERRED]**.

### 4.3 Maximum range

The range must be shorter than 31 days. 30 days 23 hours 30 minutes is served; exactly 31 days returns HTTP 400 with the message "The date range you have specified is greater than 31 days. Please select a smaller date range." **[API]** The API definition says 14 days and the website's download tool says 30 days. **[DOC]**

### 4.4 Earliest date

The first period served is 2017-09-11T23:00Z to 23:30Z, which is midnight UK local time on 12 September 2017. A range ending at 2017-09-11T00:00Z returns an empty list. **[API]** The first whole UTC hour is therefore 2017-09-11T23:00Z and the first whole UTC day is 2017-09-12.

The website says its CSV download is only available after 2017-09-26 **[DOC]**; that is presumably where the date in design section 5.1 came from **[INFERRED]**. The API serves two weeks more.

### 4.5 Missing, late and revised actual values

The half hours around the time of the spike were requested repeatedly, each time as part of a range. Each cell is forecast/actual as served at that moment; the column heads are the start of the half hour. **[API]**

| Request sent | 08:30Z | 09:00Z | 09:30Z | 10:00Z | 10:30Z |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10:03:41Z | 259/254 | 254/249 | 250/null | 242/null | |
| 10:11:38Z | 259/254 | 254/249 | 250/null | 242/null | 238/null |
| 10:15:55Z | 259/254 | 254/248 | 250/246 | 242/null | 238/null |
| 10:42:00Z | 259/254 | 254/248 | 250/246 | 242/null | 238/null |
| 10:43:30Z | 259/254 | 254/248 | 250/246 | 242/243 | 238/null |
| 11:12:00Z | 259/254 | 254/248 | 250/246 | 242/243 | 240/null |
| 11:13:30Z | 259/254 | 254/248 | 250/246 | 242/244 | 240/242 |

- **Missing.** A missing actual is `"actual": null` with `forecast` still present. In this spike it appeared only for the half hour just ended and for later ones. None of 3,960 past half hours had a null actual (914 in September 2017, 1,487 in August 2026, 1,104 in October 2026, 453 in November 2026, and two at month boundaries). **[API]** Whether other years have gaps is not known.
- **Publication delay.** An actual first appears 12 to 14 minutes after its half hour ends. The 10:00Z half hour, which ended at 10:30:00Z, was null at 10:42:00Z and 243 at 10:43:30Z. The 10:30Z half hour was null at 11:12:00Z and 242 at 11:13:30Z. Both appeared between 12.0 and 13.5 minutes after their end. The 09:30Z half hour was null at 11.6 minutes and present at 15.9 minutes, which fits. **[API]**, three publication cycles.
- **Revision.** A value can change once more when the next half hour is published, about 43 minutes after its own half hour ended. 09:00Z went from 249 to 248 between 41.6 and 45.9 minutes after its end, and 10:00Z from 243 to 244 between 42.0 and 43.5 minutes. 09:30Z stayed at 246 at the same point. Both changes were 1 gCO2/kWh. Nothing changed after that second publication: 09:00Z stayed at 248 for the next 58 minutes, 09:30Z stayed at 246 through two later cycles, and a request for 00:00Z to 10:30Z repeated 70 minutes after the first returned the same values for all 18 half hours that were more than an hour old the first time. **[API]** That a value is settled after its second publication, about 45 minutes after the half hour ends, is **[INFERRED]** from these three cycles.
- **What cannot be verified.** The API serves only the current value of a period. It keeps no history, version or publication time, so a revision can be seen only by recording values at different times, and this spike covers 70 minutes. NESO's documentation says only that the estimated intensity is provided at the end of each half hour settlement period; it says nothing about later revisions or a settlement timetable. **[DOC]** Whether values change hours or days later is not known (section 9).
- **`/intensity` is not reliable for the latest period.** At 10:02:25Z it returned the half hour 09:30Z to 10:00Z, not the one in progress, with an actual of 249. The range endpoint had null for that half hour at the time and 246 later; 249 was then the actual of the half hour before. **[API]** Use it as a reachability probe only.

### 4.6 Forecast and actual in one response, and a preliminary flag

- A national period carries both values at once. `forecast` and `actual` are separate properties of the same `intensity` object, and `forecast` stays in place after the actual is published. A source can always tell which of the two it used. **[API]**
- `forecast` is itself updated until about real time. The forecast for 10:30Z read 238 thirteen minutes into that half hour and 240 after it had ended; the forecast for 11:00Z moved from 233 to 236 over the same interval. Forecasts of half hours that had already ended did not change in any capture. **[API]**
- Nothing marks an actual as preliminary, revised or final. The body has `from`, `to`, `forecast`, `actual` and `index` and nothing else, and none of the responses had an `ETag`, `Last-Modified` or cache header. The 248 that replaced 249 looks like any other value. **[API]**
- So a preliminary flag cannot be read from the response. The source has to derive it, and two inputs are available **[INFERRED]**:
  1. Which property the value came from. A point built from `forecast` because `actual` was null is preliminary, and estimated.
  2. The age of the period when it was fetched. `IntensityPoint` already carries `periodEnd` and `retrievedAt`. An actual fetched less than a settling time after its period ended is preliminary; one fetched later counts as settled. On this spike's evidence the settling time is about 45 minutes after the half hour ends, so an hourly point is settled about 45 minutes after its hour ends. One hour is the smallest safe figure, and a longer default costs little.
- The regional series has no actual, so no outturn ever settles a regional point. A regional value for a period that has not ended is a forecast that NESO updates every 30 minutes **[DOC]**. Regional values also go on changing after their half hour has ended, and for longer than national ones: London's 09:00Z value read 268 at 46 minutes after its end and 261 an hour later, and 09:30Z went from 263 to 255 over the same hour, while 08:30Z did not change between 72 and 134 minutes after its end. **[API]**, one pair of captures. A regional point is therefore preliminary for at least 46 minutes after its period, and estimated for good.
- A consequence for the point flag proposed in ADR 0025 (status Proposed, on trunk since 2026-11-10): the intensity store keeps a point for good. `missingPeriods` counts a period with a stored point as present, so a preliminary point that has been stored is never fetched again, and a refresh of cached estimates would read the same stored value. Either preliminary points are not stored, or the resolver treats a stored preliminary point as missing once its settling time has gone by. Until one of those exists, the hold-back in 8.2 does the same job by not asking for young hours at all.

### 4.7 Data quality

- One actual of 0 was found, at 2026-08-06T11:00Z, between 34 and 41, with a forecast of 62. The generation mix for that half hour has 7.4 % gas and 8.1 % biomass, which alone give about 39 gCO2/kWh with NESO's factors. It is taken to be an artefact. **[INFERRED]** The half hour before it reads 34 after 61 and may be affected too. The design's bounds check (0 to 1,500) does not catch a 0.
- The forecast can be far off for single periods: 5 against an actual of 169 at 2026-08-23T20:30Z. **[API]**
- Mean absolute difference between forecast and actual, per half hour: 15.5 gCO2/kWh in September 2017 (6 % of the mean actual), 12.4 in August 2026 (10 %), 10.4 in the first half of October 2026 (9 %), 7.2 from 24 October to 10 November 2026 (4 %). This is what filling a missing actual from the forecast would cost. **[API]**, computed.

### 5.2 Fields and shapes

A regional period carries the intensity and the region's generation mix:

```json
{
  "from": "2026-11-03T00:00Z",
  "to": "2026-11-03T00:30Z",
  "intensity": { "forecast": 101, "index": "moderate" },
  "generationmix": [{ "fuel": "biomass", "perc": 8.2 }]
}
```

The wrapper differs by endpoint. **[API]**

- `/regional` and `/regional/intensity/{from}/{to}`: `data` is a list of periods, each `{from, to, regions}`, where each region is `{regionid, dnoregion, shortname, intensity, generationmix}`.
- `/regional/intensity/{from}/{to}/regionid/{regionid}`: `data` is one object `{regionid, dnoregion, shortname, data}` and the inner `data` is the list of periods.
- `/regional/intensity/{from}/{to}/postcode/{postcode}`: the same, with `postcode` added and `dnoregion` absent.
- `/regional/regionid/{regionid}`, `/regional/england` and `/regional/postcode/{postcode}` (current half hour): `data` is a list holding one such object.

The period convention is the same as the national one, including the extra leading period on aligned requests. **[API]** Regional forecasts are integers; across regions they ranged from 0 (North Scotland) to 351 (South West England) in one half hour on 2026-11-10. **[API]**

### 5.3 Forecast only

- No regional response had an `actual` property: not for the current half hour, not for a past hour a week old in all 18 regions, not in 31 days of October 2026 for London, not in May 2018. **[API]**
- The API definition's "Regional From-To" and "Regional ID" schemas list `forecast` and `index` under `intensity`. The national "Intensity" schema lists `forecast`, `actual` and `index`. **[DOC]**
- The regional methodology paper is titled a forecast methodology and describes forecasts for the regions up to 48 hours ahead. It says demand and generation per region are forecast with machine learning models, that the forecasts are updated every 30 minutes with a nowcasting step, and that the estimate of regional generation uses metered data. It describes no outturn series. **[DOC]**
- The national methodology paper says estimated carbon intensity is provided at the end of each settlement period, from metered generation. That is the `actual` field, and it exists for the national figure only. **[DOC]**

### 5.4 What the past regional values are

672 half hours, 2026-10-01T00:00Z to 2026-10-15T00:00Z, computed from recorded responses:

| Series | Mean gCO2/kWh | Mean absolute difference from the national actual: half-hourly, hourly, daily |
|---|---|---|
| National actual | 114.4 | |
| National forecast | 111.6 | 10.6, 9.8, 3.8 |
| Regional GB aggregate (18) | 113.5 | 3.9, 3.8, 2.6 |
| Regional London (13) | 142.7 | 30.8, 30.7, 28.3 |

Two readings, both **[INFERRED]**:

1. The value the regional series holds for a past half hour is not a day-ahead forecast. The GB aggregate tracks the national actual almost three times more closely than the national forecast does, and regional values were seen to change after their half hour had ended (4.6). That fits the methodology's 30 minute nowcast updates and its use of metered generation: the stored value is presumably an estimate that goes on being corrected for a while after real time. It is still a model output with no outturn to check a single region against, and the closeness of the GB total says nothing about how well the model splits it between regions.
2. Location matters more than forecast against actual. London read 25 % above the national actual on average over the fortnight, with a largest half-hourly difference of 113 gCO2/kWh. In the half hour 09:30Z on 2026-11-10 the GB aggregate was 250, London 263, South England 296 and South Wales 348. **[API]**

- A 31 day range for one region is served, so the regional limit is looser than the national one, or absent. **[API]**
- At 10:16Z on 2026-11-10 both the national and the regional series ended at 2026-11-12T13:00Z, about 51 hours ahead. **[API]** The website speaks of 96 hours and more **[DOC]**; the API did not serve that.

## 6. Generation mix

- `GET /generation/{from}/{to}` returns `data` as a list of `{from, to, generationmix}`; `generationmix` is a list of `{fuel, perc}`. `GET /generation` returns `data` as a single object, not a list. **[API]**
- National fuels: `biomass`, `coal`, `imports`, `gas`, `nuclear`, `other`, `hydro`, `solar`, `wind`. `perc` is a percentage with one decimal; the nine values summed to between 99.8 and 100.2 over 2,881 periods. **[API]**
- Every regional period carries the region's own `generationmix` with the same nine fuels, so there is no separate regional mix endpoint. **[API]** The API definition's regional sample also shows a `storage` fuel **[DOC]**; it was not seen live.
- The mix is percentages only, with no MW or demand figure. Intensity cannot be rebuilt from it exactly: `gas` merges combined cycle and open cycle plant and `imports` merges the interconnectors, and NESO applies a different factor to each (`/intensity/factors`). Use the intensity endpoints for intensity and the mix for reporting only. **[INFERRED]**
- The generation mix and regional sections of the API definition are marked beta. **[DOC]**
- The earliest date of the mix series was not checked.

## 7. Licence, terms and attribution

### 7.1 Licence

> Contains data from the Carbon Intensity API by National Energy System Operator (NESO), https://carbonintensity.org.uk, licensed under CC BY 4.0 (https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/). Half-hourly values averaged to hours.

### 7.3 Terms of use that affect the source

From the terms of use. **[DOC]**

- NESO applies a rate limit and may block applications that make a large number of calls. No number is given. The 52 requests of this spike, a second or more apart, were not throttled. **[API]**
- The terms forbid concealing your own or the application's identity when requesting authorisation to use the API. There is no authorisation step, and the spike used curl's default User-Agent, as instructed. In the spirit of the clause the source should send a User-Agent that names the application and its version **[INFERRED]**.
- No selling or sublicensing of access to the API, and no application that replaces NESO's own. Neither applies to an estimator that stores and redistributes derived data under CC BY 4.0.
- The terms ask for written approval before any use of NESO's word mark or logo. Naming NESO in an attribution line is what CC BY 4.0 requires and uses no logo, but the owner should know the clause exists. This is not legal advice.
- The API is provided as is and may change or stop without notice.

### 7.4 What the numbers are

From the methodology papers and the factors endpoint. **[DOC]** and **[API]**

- The national figure is generation per fuel times a factor per fuel, plus interconnector imports at a factor per interconnector, divided by national demand and corrected for transmission losses "to give the intensity of consumption" (national methodology). The website describes it as CO2 per kilowatt hour consumed. The regional figure is the intensity of electricity consumed in each region, from a power flow model of a reduced GB network.
- Import factors come from each neighbour's average generation mix over the previous 24 hours, collected daily, with fixed fallback values. Imports are counted but not traced beyond the neighbour.
- The factors are direct CO2 from generation: 0 for nuclear, hydro, wind, solar and pumped storage, 394 for combined cycle gas, 937 for coal. Upstream and other greenhouse gas emissions are excluded by NESO's own statement. Biomass is the exception at 120, a consumption-based accounting figure.
- Embedded generators NESO cannot see are not counted.

So under the design's vocabulary both series are `location-consumption` with `factorType: 'direct'`, and the values are gCO2/kWh, not gCO2e/kWh. Design sections 5.1, 5.2 and 15 call NESO production-based.

## 8. Recommendations

### 8.1 Which series the GB cloud regions should resolve to

| Choice | Zone | `basis` | `factorType` | `isEstimated` | Tier, hourly usage | Tier, daily or longer usage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| National actual | `national` | `location-consumption` | `direct` | `false` | A | B |
| National forecast, for hours with no actual (not recommended, see 8.2) | `national` | `location-consumption` | `direct` | `true` | C | C |
| Regional series (a DNO region or an aggregate) | `region-<id>` | `location-consumption` | `direct` | `true` | C | C |

The tiers follow section 6.4 and `deriveQualityTier`: an estimated value is tier C at any usage granularity. Hourly usage arrives with Phase 3, so until then the national actual gives tier B. A regional value is marked estimated because it is a forecast with no outturn behind it, by NESO's own description.

**Recommendation: resolve AWS `eu-west-2`, Azure `uksouth` and `ukwest`, and GCP `europe-west2` to the national actual series.** The basis:

1. It is the only NESO series with outturn values, so it is the only one that reaches tier A and B. Section 6.3 ("tier A or B in GB") and the Phase 2 acceptance criterion (tier B for all GB regions on a daily run) both assume that; a regional mapping would make every GB row tier C.
2. The region a provider's data centres sit in cannot be verified. AWS, Microsoft and Google name a metropolitan area and nothing finer (section 8.3). Data centres sold as London are spread over several DNO regions **[INFERRED]**: Slough resolves to region 12, not 13 **[API]**. The choice between neighbouring regions moved the answer by 33 gCO2/kWh in one half hour (London 263, South England 296), so an unverifiable mapping would add an error of the size it is meant to remove.
3. It matches what the other sources give for the same regions. The three provider packages already map all four regions to the one Electricity Maps zone `GB` (`AWSRegions.ts`, `AzureRegions.ts`, `GCPRegions.ts`). The provider carbon reports used for calibration in section 10 are also not broken down below the national grid **[INFERRED]**, not checked in this spike.

What is given up: the locational signal. If NESO's regional model is right, electricity consumed in London was about a quarter more carbon intensive than the national average in early October 2026, and the national figure understates it. That is a known limitation to state in the methodology page. If the owner later wants the regional series, it should be an explicit opt-in that keeps the tier C label; ranking a forecast-only series above C is a change to the tier definitions in section 6.4 and needs its own decision.

For scale: the national actual averaged 114 gCO2/kWh in the first half of October 2026 and 171 from 24 October to 10 November. The static factors CCF applies today are 305 for AWS `eu-west-2`, 225 for both Azure regions and 106 for GCP `europe-west2` (grid). **[API]** and the provider constants files.

### 8.2 Mapping half-hourly points to hours

The resolver takes whole UTC hours, days, months or years and drops anything else (`usablePoints`), and design section 2 already says sub-hourly intensity is aggregated to the hour. The source should produce hourly points as follows.

1. Request `GET /intensity/{start}/{end}` with `start` and `end` on UTC hour boundaries, at most 14 days per request. 14 days is the documented maximum; the service accepts more today, but the documented figure is the one NESO has committed to. A year is 27 requests of about 73 KiB.
2. Keep the periods with `from >= start` and `to <= end`. This drops the extra leading period and does not depend on the one-minute behaviour in 4.2.
3. Check that each kept period is 30 minutes long and starts at minute 0 or 30.
4. The point for the hour starting at H is the mean of `actual` for the periods starting at H:00 and H:30. The halves are equal in length, so the plain mean is the time-weighted mean the resolver expects. The API gives no demand figure to weight by energy; the two halves of an hour differed by 6 gCO2/kWh on average and by 16 at the 95th percentile in the October sample, so the effect is small **[INFERRED]**. Values are integers, so hourly means can end in .5.
5. Emit a point only when both halves have an actual, with `isEstimated: false`. Treat an actual of 0 as missing (4.7); a real zero is not possible while biomass and gas run, and the rule can be revisited when that changes.
6. Emit nothing for an hour with a missing half. The resolver then sees partial coverage for that usage period, marks it estimated (tier C), stores nothing for the hour and asks again on a later run. Do not fill the hour from `forecast` and store it: the store never refetches a period it holds, so a stored forecast would never be replaced by the actual.
7. Declare `granularities: ['hour']` only. The resolver then requests hours for daily and longer usage too and time-weights them itself, and the row is tier B. Declaring `day` as well would make the source average 48 half hours for no gain.
8. Report `earliest` as 2017-09-11T23:00Z and `latest` as the end of the last whole UTC hour that finished at least a hold-back ago, so that hours whose values may still change are not requested and stored. One hour covers everything seen in this spike (4.5): publication at 12 to 14 minutes and one revision at about 43 minutes. Two hours is suggested as the default, as a margin over 70 minutes of observation, held in one constant that is easy to change when section 9, point 1 is settled. Design section 12 puts the marker of ADR 0025 before the first live source. With it, the hold-back can give way to a preliminary flag on young points, given the store rule in 4.6.

The same steps apply to a regional zone, reading `forecast` and setting `isEstimated: true`. A regional zone needs a longer hold-back, because its values were still changing 46 minutes after the half hour ended (4.6); three hours is suggested.

### 8.4 Other notes for the Phase 2 source

- `probe()` can request `GET /intensity`, 192 bytes. It should not use the values.
- Fail on a body with an `error` property even when the status is 200. Treat `null` and `{"data":[]}` as no data.
- Request in sequence, never in parallel, and rely on the store: with `retention: 'unlimited'` a stored period is fetched once.
- An hour the source cannot supply is the exception. Nothing is stored for it and the resolver keeps no record of an empty answer (`GridIntensityResolver.resolveAt` fetches every missing period on every call), so each billing row for that region and period would repeat the request. The source should remember, for the capability TTL, which ranges it has already fetched in the process and answer repeats from that. This matters because NESO's rate limit is unpublished.
- Do not use the date, settlement period, `fw` or `pt` endpoints for estimation.
- The contract tests can assert the exact URLs: each fixture records the URL that produced its body.

## 9. Not verified

1. Whether NESO revises an actual value again after its second publication, hours or days later. The API keeps no history and NESO's documentation is silent, so this spike could only watch for 70 minutes; nothing more than an hour old changed in that time. A check that records one day's values an hour, a day and a week later would settle it, and with it the hold-back in 8.2 and the settling time ADR 0025 needs.
2. How long regional values go on changing after their half hour. They were still changing after 46 minutes; one value 72 minutes old did not change in the following hour.
3. Whether the national series has null actual values in years not sampled (2018 to 2025).
4. The maximum range of the regional and generation endpoints, and the rate limit.
5. The first period of the regional series for regions other than 13, and of the generation mix series.
6. Datetime formats other than `YYYY-MM-DDThh:mmZ` (seconds, offsets) were not tried.
7. Where the providers' GB data centres are. This cannot be verified from public provider documentation.
8. That provider carbon reports use a national grid factor for GB (8.1, point 3).
